
# Heat Dissipation Generative Model Experiment

This notebook implements a novel generative model based on the heat equation, offering an alternative to traditional diffusion models.

## Project Overview
The model operates on the principle of reversing a physical process: heat dissipation. Instead of adding and removing random noise (as in standard diffusion), this model:

- **Forward Process:** Blurs images using the heat equation, progressively dissolving structure and increasing entropy. This process is deterministic and physically grounded.
- **Reverse Process:** A U-Net style neural network learns to reverse this blurring, effectively restoring structure and sharpening images from their degraded states. It predicts the original clean image (`u_0`) from a blurred image (`u_t`) and its blur level (`t`).

This approach leverages the natural decay of structure under local averaging, aiming to learn the inverse of this decay.

## Key Features and Differences from Standard Diffusion
- **Physically Grounded Forward Process:** The heat equation provides a deterministic, spatially correlated smoothing process, unlike i.i.d. Gaussian noise addition.
- **Hierarchy of Detail:** Fine details decay first, while coarse structures persist longer, providing an inherent hierarchical representation during the degradation.
- **Mass Conservation:** The total pixel intensity ('mass') is conserved throughout the forward blurring process, meaning no information is added or removed, only redistributed.
- **Deterministic Endpoint:** The forward process naturally leads to a uniform, flat field as `t` approaches infinity, rather than pure random noise.

## Dataset
The model was trained on a dynamically generated **synthetic dataset** of geometric shapes with color gradients. These images were designed to have clear spatial structure, making them ideal for observing the effects of heat dissipation and the model's ability to restore structure.

## Model Architecture
A `SimpleUNet` architecture is used for the reverse process. It takes a blurred image (`u_t`) and a sinusoidal embedding of the current timestep (`t`) as input, and predicts the original un-blurred image (`u_0`). The network uses residual blocks, group normalization, and sinusoidal positional embeddings for time conditioning.

## Training Details
- **Optimizer:** AdamW
- **Learning Rate:** 2e-04
- **Epochs:** 100
- **Batch Size:** 64
- **Timesteps (Blur Levels):** 30
- **Max Blur Strength (Alpha Max):** 10
- **Loss Function:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted original image and the actual original image.

## Results and Visualizations
During and after training, several visualizations are generated to illustrate the model's performance:

1.  **Forward Process Visualization (`forward_process.png`):** Shows how sample images progressively blur and lose detail as the heat equation is applied over increasing timesteps.
2.  **Training Loss Curve (`training_loss.png`):** Plots the MSE loss over epochs, demonstrating the model's learning progress. A decreasing curve indicates successful training.
3.  **Reconstruction Quality (`reconstruction_quality.png`):** Compares original images with their reconstructions starting from various blur levels, showcasing the model's ability to recover detail.
4.  **Final Generated Images (`gen_from_blur_epoch_XXX.png`, `gen_from_flat_epoch_XXX.png`):**
    - **From Max-Blur Seeds:** Images generated by iteratively sharpening heavily blurred versions of real images.
    - **From Flat Start:** Images generated by starting the reverse process from a near-uniform (flat) field, demonstrating the model's ability to synthesize structure from minimal initial information.
5.  **Detail Levels (`detail_levels_epoch_XXX.png`):** Illustrates the progressive build-up of detail during the reverse process, from abstract coarse structures to full detail, by stopping the reversal at different timesteps.

## Analysis
- **Mass Conservation Check:** Verification that the total pixel intensity remains constant throughout the forward blurring process, confirming the physical accuracy of the heat equation implementation.
- **Frequency Analysis:** Demonstrates that high-frequency components (fine details) decay much faster than low-frequency components (coarse structures) under heat dissipation, a key characteristic exploited by the model.
